Why Dry Season Is the Best Time to Visit Kidepo

Safari Guide — June 2026

Thinner grass, concentrated wildlife around waterholes, and the clearest skies of the year — here’s how to plan around it.

Tucked away in Uganda’s Karamoja region, Kidepo Valley National Park runs on its own climate calendar. While most of Uganda splits neatly into two rainy and two dry seasons, Kidepo’s semi-arid landscape means the dry season stretches out — roughly from September through March — and it’s when the park truly comes alive for visitors.

Wildlife Comes to You

As the rains fade, the vegetation thins and Kidepo’s rivers — the Kidepo and the Narus — shrink to scattered pools. Animals that would otherwise be spread across the park’s 1,442 square kilometers start converging on the few remaining water sources, especially in the Narus Valley, the park’s dry-season game-viewing hub.

That means less driving, less guessing, and far better odds of spotting lions resting on rocky outcrops, buffalo herds at the waterholes, and the cheetahs and ostriches Kidepo is known for.

Clearer Skies, Easier Roads

Dry season also means firmer, more navigable roads — a real advantage in a park this remote, where getting around already takes some commitment. Skies stay largely clear, giving you better visibility for game drives and sharper photos of Kidepo’s dramatic savanna framed by distant mountain ranges.

Two Windows Within the Dry Season

Not all dry months feel the same:

  • September to November — the sweet spot. Good weather, strong wildlife concentration, without the harshest heat.
  • December to February — peak dryness. The best wildlife sightings of the year, but expect dust and daytime temperatures that can climb well above 30°C.

Both windows deliver strong game viewing — it’s really a choice between comfort and sheer wildlife density.

Planning Around It

If wildlife viewing is your priority, aim for the dry season. If you’re drawn to birdwatching or lush green landscapes instead, Kidepo’s wetter months (April–August) bring migratory birds and a completely different side of the park — just be ready for muddier tracks.
