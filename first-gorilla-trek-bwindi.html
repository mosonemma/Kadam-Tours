What to Expect on Your First Gorilla Trek in Bwindi

Trekking — February 2026

Permit rules, fitness prep, and the moment you finally meet the family group.

Nothing quite prepares you for it, but knowing what’s coming makes the day easier to enjoy rather than just survive. Here’s what a first gorilla trek in Bwindi Impenetrable National Park actually involves.

The Permit

A gorilla permit is issued by the Uganda Wildlife Authority and is your only way into Bwindi or Mgahinga to track the gorillas — only eight visitors are allowed per gorilla family per day, which keeps demand high and availability tight. Peak season runs June to September and December to February, so booking well ahead matters, even in the quieter rainy months.

The minimum age is strictly 15, and you’ll need to bring identification for age verification on the day.

Choosing Your Sector

Bwindi is split into four trekking sectors, and your permit locks you into one:

  • Buhoma (north) — the most established sector, generally lower altitude and sometimes gentler terrain
  • Ruhija (east) — quieter and higher altitude, known for its views and birdwatching
  • Rushaga (south) — home to the largest number of habituated families and the Gorilla Habituation Experience
  • Nkuringo (southwest) — dramatic views, but a tougher, more demanding trek

First-timers are usually steered toward Buhoma for the gentlest introduction, but a good operator will match your sector to your fitness and what you actually want out of the day.

Fitness Prep

You don’t need to be an athlete, but don’t underestimate it either. Most trekkers should be comfortable walking two to six hours on uneven forest trails, and the terrain can mean steep, muddy ground, slippery logs, and stream crossings, especially if it’s rained. Some regular pre-trip conditioning — hiking, cardio, leg strength work — makes a real difference, and a trekking pole and broken-in waterproof boots are worth the effort of packing.

Be honest about your fitness level at the morning briefing — rangers use that information to match you with a gorilla family whose location suits your ability, so downplaying it doesn’t do you any favors.

What to Pack

  • Sturdy, waterproof hiking boots with good ankle support
  • Long-sleeved shirt and long trousers — protection against thorns, nettles, and insects along the trail
  • A pair of gardening or work gloves for gripping muddy slopes and vegetation
  • Lightweight rain jacket, hat, and insect repellent
  • A daypack with water, snacks, and a camera — skip the flash, since it can startle the gorillas

The Day Itself

The day starts early with a briefing at Uganda Wildlife Authority headquarters, where rangers cover park rules and what to expect before assigning groups to specific gorilla families. From there, the actual hike can take anywhere from thirty minutes to several hours, depending entirely on where that family happens to be that morning.

Meeting the Gorillas

Once you find them, park rules require staying at least 7 meters away at all times, with no loud noises or flash photography. You’ll get roughly forty minutes to an hour with the family — feeding, grooming, moving through the undergrowth, sometimes closer than you’d expect. It goes fast. Most people say the same thing afterward: however much they braced for the hike, nothing quite braced them for that hour.
