A First-Timer’s Guide to Trekking Mount Moroto

Trekking — May 2026

What to pack, how to pace the ridge climb, and where to catch the best sunrise.

Rising above the town of Moroto in Uganda’s Karamoja region, Mount Moroto stands at roughly 3,083 meters, making it the highest peak in the region — an extinct volcano part of the same chain of mountains along the Kenya-Uganda border that includes Mount Elgon, Mount Kadam, and Mount Morungole. For a first ridge climb into Karamoja’s highlands, it’s hard to beat.

Before You Go

You don’t need technical climbing gear or prior mountaineering experience to take this on — the terrain shifts from dry thorn savanna at the base to montane woodland and rocky outcrops higher up, but the trails themselves are walkable with the right footwear and fitness. A local guide is strongly recommended — not just for safety on unfamiliar terrain, but to bridge the language gap with communities along the trail who mostly speak their native language.

When to go: The dry season is the best window for hiking Moroto and the other Karamoja mountain ranges — expect firmer trails and clearer views.

What to pack: - Sturdy hiking boots (loose rock and roots are common underfoot on the lower ridges) - Sun protection — hats, sunscreen, and plenty of water, since the sun intensifies fast once it’s up - A warm layer for camping on the ridge, where nights get noticeably cooler than the valley floor - Basic camping gear if doing an overnight route (tents, mats, and sleeping bags are usually included in guided overnight packages, though it’s worth confirming with your operator)

Pacing the Climb

Most routes begin outside Moroto town, following a footpath that opens onto flat homestead land and sunflower fields before the ridgeline climbing starts in earnest. Starting early is genuinely worth it — the heat builds quickly once the sun is fully up.

The climb itself comes in stages: a steady ascent through terraced homesteads, then a scramble over exposed rock and through deep grass as you gain the ridge. Staying close to your guide matters here — it’s easy to lose the trail and end up descending the wrong side of the ridge. Many first-timers break the trek into two days, camping partway up before pushing for the summit.

What You’ll See Along the Way

This isn’t just a physical challenge — it’s a cultural and ecological one too. Mount Moroto is home to the Tepeth people, the region’s earliest known inhabitants, who moved up the mountain as the Karamojong settled the lowlands below. The mountain’s varied ecosystem also supports plant species that hold traditional medicinal and nutritional value for local communities.

Wildlife sightings are a bonus rather than a guarantee, but keep an eye out — hikers have spotted golden cats, monkeys, and some of the roughly 220 recorded bird species along the trails.

The Sunrise Payoff

The reward for an early camp and a pre-dawn push: ridge-top views stretching across Karamoja’s savanna, with Kenya’s landscape visible from the higher peaks on a clear morning. It’s the kind of first-light view that’s easy to describe and hard to actually capture — worth the early alarm.
